Regulatory Framework

RBI guidelines for outward remittance from India

Outward remittance is the transfer of foreign exchange by an Indian resident to a beneficiary outside India, except for Nepal and Bhutan, for a purpose permitted under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA).

FTH Wire Services operates as an RBI-authorised Dealer, Category II (AD-II), and can process outward remittances for approved purposes. Transfers may be processed through telegraphic transfer or demand draft, subject to the applicable regulations and limits under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS).

KYC and Compliance

Before an outward remittance is processed, the sender's KYC must be verified. At our branches, the designated operations and management staff verify the original documents and certify the KYC as part of the process — helping ensure the transaction is processed in line with applicable requirements.

Can You Send Money Abroad from India?

Yes. Indian residents can send money abroad for permitted purposes, subject to applicable FEMA and RBI rules. Requirements vary by the sender's residential status and the purpose of transfer; business transactions relating to import and export of goods and services are restricted to authorised banks.

Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, introduced on February 4, 2004, resident individuals can remit money abroad for permitted current and capital account transactions. The applicable limit is currently USD 2.5 lakh, or its equivalent, per financial year.
